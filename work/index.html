0. Abstract

FlyRank content teams may manage many pages while having limited SEO and editorial review time, creating a practical need to decide which pages deserve attention first. This study tests whether observed search-performance signals can help rank content pages for CTR-focused human review using pseudonymized FlyRank warehouse data, with March 2026 used as the feature period and April 2026 as a later evaluation period. I compare a transparent position-adjusted CTR baseline with a Random Forest model using March impressions, clicks, CTR, average search position, position volatility, and active search days, evaluated on clients completely held out from training. Both approaches achieved 0.880 Precision@50, substantially above the held-out opportunity base rate of 0.215, while the Random Forest showed stronger broader ranking performance with Average Precision of 0.610 versus 0.555 and ROC-AUC of 0.864 versus 0.795. The output is a directional, human-review prioritization queue designed to help editors decide which pages to inspect first rather than automatically changing content.

1. Introduction / Problem statement

A content team may have hundreds or thousands of pages but limited time available for SEO review. Reviewing every page equally is inefficient, while a single universal CTR threshold can be misleading because expected CTR depends on ranking position, search visibility, query intent, SERP conditions, and evidence volume.

The practical FlyRank question is therefore not simply:

Which pages have low CTR?

Instead, this capstone asks:

Which content pages should a FlyRank SEO specialist or content editor inspect first when review capacity is limited?

Research question

Among content pages with enough observed search exposure, can March 2026 search-performance signals be used to rank pages associated with a later position-adjusted CTR-opportunity proxy measured in April 2026?

Decision supported

The unit of analysis is one pseudonymized client-content item. The output is a continuous opportunity score and ranked review queue. A human editor can inspect high-ranked pages first and decide whether title, meta description, search-result presentation, intent alignment, ranking movement, or other contextual factors justify action.

Cost of a wrong call

A false positive can waste editorial time or encourage an unnecessary change. A false negative can push a potentially useful review opportunity too far down the queue. Because review capacity is limited, precision near the top of the ranking is the main operational concern.

2. Data

The analysis uses the approved pseudonymized FlyRank ML Internship warehouse release. The primary source is fact_content_daily_performance. Aggregation is performed in DuckDB, and only the smaller per-content feature frame is brought into pandas for modeling.

Time windows

  • Feature period: March 1–31, 2026
  • Outcome period: April 1–30, 2026

Eligibility

  • At least 500 March impressions
  • Measurable March average position
  • At least 500 April impressions for retrospective evaluation
  • Measurable April average position

Predictive fields used

  • log_impressions
  • log_clicks
  • feature_ctr
  • feature_avg_position
  • feature_position_std
  • feature_active_days

Fields deliberately excluded

Client/content IDs are used only for joins, grouping, and validation. April outcome fields, the target proxy, product decision flags, raw client names, domains, URLs, page titles, keywords, and private search queries are excluded from the model.

Public-safety constraint The deployed paper contains no raw client names, URLs, domains, private queries, or authentication tokens.

3. Methodology

Assumptions

  1. March performance contains useful information about later review opportunity, without implying causality.
  2. Pages in similar search-position bands are more comparable than pages evaluated under one universal CTR threshold.
  3. The 500-impression threshold reduces low-volume noise but is a practical analysis choice, not a universal SEO rule.
  4. The April proxy is a review-prioritization proxy, not objective content quality or proof that editing is needed.
  5. Holding out complete clients is a stronger test of generalization than randomly holding out pages from familiar clients.
  6. Important unobserved factors remain, including exact SERP composition, competitors, brand effects, detailed search intent, and seasonality.
  7. The study is observational rather than causal.

Position bands

Pages are compared in broad ranking groups: positions 1–3, 4–10, 11–20, 21–50, and deeper than 50.

Outcome proxy

Each page is compared with the median April CTR of pages in its broad April position band. A page receives opportunity_proxy = 1 when its April CTR is more than 0.10 percentage points below that peer median.

Transparent baseline

The baseline score is:

positive March peer CTR gap × log(1 + March impressions)

This favors pages with both meaningful search exposure and CTR below pages in similar search-position bands.

Random Forest model

The learned model is a Random Forest classifier using six March-only features. Predicted probabilities are used as ranking scores. The model is not used as an automatic editing or publishing decision.

Validation design

Final evaluation uses GroupShuffleSplit by client_hash_id, with approximately 75% of clients in training and 25% completely held out for testing. No client appears in both partitions.

Leakage checks

All model features end on March 31 and the outcome begins on April 1. April-derived fields, the proxy, pseudonymous IDs, and product decision fields are excluded from the predictive feature set.

Deliberate leakage stress test A deliberately leaky model reached 1.000 Precision@50 and 1.000 Average Precision. That result is a diagnostic demonstrating how target-related future information can create artificially perfect performance; it is not reported as valid model performance.

Random seed: 42.

Headline result

The Random Forest did not improve the operational top-20 or top-50 queue: both methods achieved 0.850 Precision@20 and 0.880 Precision@50. However, the Random Forest performed better across the broader ranking, with Average Precision rising from 0.555 to 0.610 and ROC-AUC from 0.795 to 0.864.

The practical finding is therefore not that machine learning clearly beats the rule baseline. Rather, the Random Forest improves broader ranking discrimination while the transparent baseline performs equally well for the immediate top-of-queue decision.

Error analysis

False positives may occur when ranking conditions, SERP composition, intent, or seasonality change between March and April. Missed opportunities may occur when March looks normal but April search conditions shift. The proxy also cannot represent every editorial or business consideration.

5. Limitations & honest framing

This study is designed as observational decision-support. It does not estimate the causal effect of changing a title, meta description, page content, or any other SEO element.

  • The April proxy is not true content quality. It is a position-adjusted CTR-opportunity definition used to prioritize review.
  • No causal claim is supported. A high score does not mean an edit will improve CTR, traffic, or ranking.
  • Important context is unobserved. Exact SERP layout, competitor changes, brand effects, detailed query intent, and external events may affect CTR.
  • Seasonality and temporal drift may matter. Relationships observed in March/April 2026 may change in future periods.
  • Generalization is tested across held-out clients, not guaranteed for every future client.
  • The 500-impression threshold is a practical choice. It reduces low-volume noise but may exclude legitimate low-traffic opportunities.
  • Human review remains necessary. The model does not observe enough context to authorize publishing, deletion, redirect, canonical, or other content/technical changes automatically.
Claim level The results are reported as observed, measured, directional, and decision-support evidence.

6. Ranked recommendations

The operational output is a ranked queue for human review. High-ranked pages can be paired with transparent reason codes such as:

  • high_visibility
  • below_position_peer_ctr
  • strong_position_ctr_gap
  • position_volatile
  • model_priority_only

Action playbook

Review title, meta, and intent: use when a page has meaningful visibility, a relatively strong position, and CTR below comparable pages.

Review snippet and query fit: use when the main signal is a position-adjusted CTR gap.

Monitor position before editing: use when ranking position is volatile and CTR may be reflecting movement rather than a stable page-level issue.

Manual review only: use when the model assigns high priority but the transparent rule does not support a clear editing hypothesis.

Which ranking method should be used?

For a small operational queue such as the top 20 or top 50 pages, the transparent baseline is a strong default because it matched the Random Forest's Precision@K while remaining easier to explain and audit. The Random Forest may be more useful when ranking a larger inventory because it showed stronger Average Precision and ROC-AUC across the full held-out population.

No-go actions

The system should never automatically rewrite or publish content, delete or redirect pages, deindex pages, alter canonical tags, make technical SEO changes, or claim that a specific edit will improve CTR.
